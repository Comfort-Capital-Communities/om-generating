The Premise

It’s not what you make. It’s what you keep.

Two investments can earn the exact same pre-tax return and leave you in completely different places. The difference isn’t the headline number — it’s how much of it survives the tax bill and stays invested.

Most of our investors have already figured out how to earn. This presentation is about keeping more of that capital working — and letting it compound.

The “lazy exchange”

Traditional
1031 Exchange
  • 1Sell the property
  • 2Proceeds go to a qualified intermediary
  • 3Identify replacement property on a clock
  • 4Close on the replacement
  • 5Defer the tax
Alternative
“Lazy Exchange”
  • 1Sell the rental — recognize the gain
  • 2Reinvest in fresh depreciable rental real estate the same tax year
  • 3Generate new depreciation
  • 4Use qualifying rental losses against qualifying rental gain
Not a Section 1031 exchange

Neither is always better — the point is you have more options than “sell and pay the tax.”

Cash flow does not equal taxable income

$50,000
Cash Distribution · from operations
≠
$50,000
Taxable Income · after depreciation
Real cash can arrive alongside a paper tax loss.

Distributions come from the property’s cash flow. Taxable income is calculated after depreciation — which is why much of what you receive during the hold can be sheltered, and some of it can be treated as a non-taxable return of capital rather than ordinary income.
