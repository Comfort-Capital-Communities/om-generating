Why Manufactured Housing

Five Reasons MHCs Are On The Rise

Solution to the Affordability Crisis
≈50%MH cost per sq. ft. vs. site-built
One of the most realistic answers to America’s affordability crisis: quality factory-built homes at roughly half the per-square-foot cost, already serving nearly 8 million U.S. households.
Recession-Resilient Assets
+87%MHC operating-income growth, 2004–2018
Property income has stayed positive through every recession since 2000. In 2008, MHC space rents grew 3%+ while multifamily rents fell more than 6%.
Asymmetric, Resilient Returns
7–10%CapEx as a % of NOI, among the lowest in real estate
Land-based ownership and low capital needs have delivered strong historical returns with low volatility, a rare risk/return profile among core real-estate sectors.
Superior Tax Advantages
15-yrAccelerated depreciation schedule
MHCs depreciate on an accelerated 15-year schedule versus 27.5–39 years for other real estate, and year-one bonus depreciation on land improvements enhances investor returns.
Limited New & Existing Supply
43,000+U.S. communities, net supply shrinking since 2000
Zoning, land cost and multi-year lease-up choke new development. With ~4.3 million homesites and net supply falling since 2000, demand for affordable lots keeps climbing.
The Most Tax-Advantaged Real Estate

Tax Benefits

Cost-Segregation Analysis
We commission detailed, engineering-based cost-segregation studies on our acquisitions to identify and reclassify components into shorter depreciation lives (5-, 7- and 15-year). This front-loads depreciation, improves projected after-tax cash flow, and sharpens our underwriting.
Bonus Depreciation
A large portion of an MHC's value sits in land improvements such as roads, utilities and pads, depreciated over 15 years. Bonus-depreciation rules allow a significant first-year deduction on those components, creating meaningful paper losses in year one.
Offset Passive Income
Depreciation is a non-cash "paper loss" that can offset passive income from this and other investments. Investors who qualify as Real Estate Professionals may be able to offset active income as well; consult your advisor.
Tax-Efficient Distributions
Because depreciation often exceeds taxable income in the early years, a meaningful share of the monthly distributions investors receive can be treated as a non-taxable return of capital rather than ordinary income.
1031 Exchanges
On disposition we frequently roll proceeds into a larger up-leg property through a 1031 exchange, deferring capital-gains tax and compounding investor capital across successive deals.
Long-Term Capital Gains
Communities held longer than a year are taxed at favorable long-term capital-gains rates upon exit, rather than higher ordinary-income rates, which enhances net, after-tax investor returns.

Why Investors Love Depreciation

Depreciation is a tax deduction that recognizes how physical assets lose value over time. In real estate it lets investors offset income by writing off part of the property's value each year, even as the property itself may rise in market value.

In manufactured-housing communities, land improvements such as roads, water lines and sewer systems depreciate over 15 years instead of the standard 27.5, capturing larger deductions earlier. Bonus depreciation writes off up to 100% of eligible improvements in year one, and even passive investors receive their share on a K-1.

Leverage amplifies the benefit. Depreciation is calculated on the community's full cost, not just the equity invested, so financing part of the purchase with debt pairs every investor dollar with additional depreciable basis, producing a larger year-one paper loss relative to the capital an investor actually contributes.

Frequently Asked

Investor FAQs

Can I invest using SDIRA / SD401K funds?
Yes, so long as your provider permits the use of funds for MHC investing and proper regulations are followed. We advise working closely with your custodian.
What is the minimum investment?
$100,000 is the minimum. Exceptions are at manager discretion.
Do you provide investor reporting?
Yes. A comprehensive quarterly Asset Management Report covering progress and performance.
When and how do I receive distributions?
Generally 3–6 months after closing. Once underway, distributions are sent monthly on the 10th via ACH direct deposit.
What info do I provide my accountant?
Investors receive a secure Juniper Square portal at onboarding. Each year your K-1 is uploaded for download and delivery to your accountant.
What happens after you sell a community?
We may consolidate neighboring assets and pursue a 1031 exchange into an up-leg property, deferring capital-gains tax while compounding investor capital.
Am I investing in a fund?
No. Each deal is structured as an individual LLC. Investors own a pro-rata share of the specific property, increasing transparency and reducing blind-pool risk.

Definitions & Calculations

How We Calculate Returns

Holding Period
Years between acquisition and exit; months are divided by 12 and added to the year count.
Equity Multiple (EM)
How much an investor could make on the initial investment. 2.25x implies $2.25 returned for every $1 invested.
Average Annual Return (AAR)
Total historical return expressed as a single annualized percentage over the holding period.
Cash-on-Cash (CoC)
Annual cash income relative to cash invested; excludes sale proceeds.
Gross Total Return
Sale proceeds plus total distributions, less equity raised, divided by equity raised. Property level.
Gross EM
Total sale proceeds plus distributions, divided by total invested. Property level; includes acquisition/management fees.
Gross AAR
Gross total return divided by the holding period, annualized before investor-level fees and promotes.
Gross CoC
Cash distributed over the investment life divided by initial cash, then by the holding period. Property level.
Gross IRR
The annualized, time-weighted return on all property-level cash flows and sale proceeds, before investor fees and promotes. Unlike AAR, it accounts for the timing of every dollar in and out.
Net Total Return
Total return at the investor level, realized after all sponsor promotes and fees.
Net EM
The equity multiple at the investor level per park, once promotes and fees are removed.
Net AAR
Net total return divided by the holding period; the annualized investor-level return, net of fees and promotes.
Net CoC
The cash-on-cash return for an individual park at the investor level, after promotes.
Net IRR
The annualized, time-weighted return an LP actually earns, after all sponsor fees and promotes. It reflects when capital is contributed and when cash is distributed, and is the headline return on the summary page.
Weighted Average EM / AAR / CoC
Each park's metric weighted by its share of total portfolio equity, summed, then divided by total equity invested.
Average Holding Period
The holding periods of all parks sold, added together and divided by the number of parks sold.
