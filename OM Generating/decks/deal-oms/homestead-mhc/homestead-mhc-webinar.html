Homestead MHC · Why Homestead

Investment Highlights

Located In The Phoenix MSA
The 10th-largest metro in the country, and a market we know intimately. Comfort Capital already sponsors and manages 17 other communities across the Phoenix MSA, giving Homestead an in-place regional team from day one.
Below-Market Rents
In-place lot rents average $641, well below comparable Phoenix communities. We believe this gap provides an opportunity to increase rents over the hold period, subject to market conditions.
Nearly All Resident-Owned Homes
Just 1 park-owned home across all 116 sites. A highly efficient, near-pure land-lease operating model with minimal home maintenance, turnover and capital-expenditure exposure.
Public Utilities, Billed Back
Public water, sewer and electric are billed back to residents, reducing ownership's utility burden and streamlining day-to-day operations.
Occupied, Income-Producing Asset
At 96% occupancy, Homestead delivers immediate in-place cash flow from day one. An occupied, income-producing acquisition rather than a heavy lease-up or operational turnaround.
Tax-Efficient Investment
Manufactured housing is among the most tax-advantaged real estate there is. A cost-segregation study paired with 100% bonus depreciation can generate a first-year paper loss of roughly 100% or more of invested equity, sheltering distributions and, for many investors, income well beyond this deal.

Homestead MHC · Strategy

Business Plan

1
Mark Rents To Market
In-place lot rents average just $641 against a comp-set market of roughly $950. We already operate 17 communities across the Phoenix MSA, and five of Homestead's thirteen direct rent comps are our own parks, so we have good optics on what we believe the market can support. Our business plan targets an average lot rent of approximately $1,125 by Year 3, subject to market conditions.
2
Targeted Capital Improvements
Deploy $807,500 of budgeted capital into roads, signage, common areas and infrastructure. These selective upgrades lift curb appeal and resident pride, support higher rents, and protect long-term value across the community.
3
Operational Efficiencies
Homestead plugs into the regional team already running our 17 nearby Phoenix communities, using the same in-house management, maintenance and sales staff. It scales onto existing infrastructure with little added overhead, and with full public-utility billbacks, that operating leverage grows NOI from day one.
4
Floor Inventory
Floor 8 homes, 4 on vacant pads and 4 replacing temporary RVs on mobile-home lots. Comfort MH, our licensed dealer, sources them directly at wholesale while selling them to residents, converting transient and vacant spaces into stabilized, lot-paying homeowner sites and pushing occupancy toward 100%.
5
Distribute Cash Flow
We target monthly distributions beginning on or about July 10, 2027 at an initial annualized rate of 5%.
6
5-Year Plan
In Year 5 we have the optionality of either selling the community or executing a cash-out refinance to return nearly 50% of capital, which is generally not taxable when distributed, depending on each investor's tax basis.

The Most Tax-Advantaged Real Estate

Tax Benefits

Cost-Segregation Analysis
We commission detailed, engineering-based cost-segregation studies on our acquisitions to identify and reclassify components into shorter depreciation lives (5-, 7- and 15-year). This front-loads depreciation, improves projected after-tax cash flow, and sharpens our underwriting.
Bonus Depreciation
A large portion of an MHC's value sits in land improvements such as roads, utilities and pads, depreciated over 15 years. Bonus-depreciation rules allow a significant first-year deduction on those components, creating meaningful paper losses in year one.
Offset Passive Income
Depreciation is a non-cash "paper loss" that can offset passive income from this and other investments. Investors who qualify as Real Estate Professionals may be able to offset active income as well; consult your advisor.
Tax-Efficient Distributions
Because depreciation often exceeds taxable income in the early years, a meaningful share of the monthly distributions investors receive can be treated as a non-taxable return of capital rather than ordinary income. Distributions that are not taxed when received reduce an investor's tax basis and may increase taxable gain at sale; consult your advisor.
1031 Exchanges
On disposition we frequently roll proceeds into a larger up-leg property through a 1031 exchange, deferring capital-gains tax and compounding investor capital across successive deals.
Long-Term Capital Gains
Communities held longer than a year are taxed at favorable long-term capital-gains rates upon exit, rather than higher ordinary-income rates, which enhances net, after-tax investor returns.

Why Investors Love Depreciation

Depreciation is a tax deduction that recognizes how physical assets lose value over time. In real estate it lets investors offset income by writing off part of the property's value each year, even as the property itself may rise in market value.

In manufactured-housing communities, land improvements such as roads, water lines and sewer systems depreciate over 15 years instead of the standard 27.5, capturing larger deductions earlier. Bonus depreciation writes off up to 100% of eligible improvements in year one, and even passive investors receive their share on a K-1. Passive investors generally may use these losses only against passive income.

Leverage amplifies the benefit. Depreciation is calculated on the community's full cost, not just the equity invested, so financing part of the purchase with debt pairs every investor dollar with additional depreciable basis, producing a larger year-one paper loss relative to the capital an investor actually contributes.
